End Behavior Graphically & End Behavior Algebraically

Learn to read the tails of a graph and predict polynomial end behavior from the leading term.

Learning targets — I can:

  • Describe the left and right end behavior of a polynomial graph.
  • Find a polynomial's degree and leading coefficient.
  • Predict end behavior from the leading term.
  • Write end behavior using infinity notation and explain my reasoning.
🌎 Why it matters: End behavior helps us predict how a graph will look far beyond the visible window and connects algebraic equations to graphs.

📖 Whole Group — Lesson Guide / Overview

Essential question: How can we determine what happens to a polynomial function as x approaches positive or negative infinity?

📉 Graphically

Look at the far-left and far-right tails of the graph. Does each rise or fall?

🧮 Algebraically

Find the leading term. Use its degree (even/odd) and coefficient sign (+/−).

👨‍🏫 Whole Group — I DO: Teacher Modeling

Example 1 — End Behavior Graphically (DeltaMath)

Look carefully at the graph below. Only the two ends determine end behavior—not the bumps in the middle.

  1. Left tail: points downward → as x → −∞, f(x) → −∞.
  2. Right tail: points downward → as x → ∞, f(x) → −∞.
✅ Correct answer: Choice 1 — both ends go down.

Example 2 — End Behavior Algebraically (DeltaMath)

Given: f(x) = 18x⁴ − 408x² + 416x + 2x⁵ + 768 − 40x³

  1. Reorder: f(x) = 2x⁵ + 18x⁴ − 40x³ − 408x² + 416x + 768.
  2. Leading term: 2x⁵.
  3. Degree: 5 → odd. Leading coefficient: +2 → positive.
  4. Rule: Odd + positive → left down, right up.
✅ Correct answer: Choice 3 — as x → −∞, f(x) → −∞; as x → ∞, f(x) → ∞.

🧭 Whole Group — Student Reference Chart

Use this chart whenever you are unsure which direction the ends go.

Memory trick: Even degree → both ends go the same way. Odd degree → ends go opposite ways. A positive leading coefficient makes the right end go up; a negative one makes it go down.
